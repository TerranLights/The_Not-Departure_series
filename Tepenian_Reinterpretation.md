# Departure — Reinterpreted for the Tepenian Universe

A tribute/continuation of the 2013 ASMR series *Departure* (creator: Ally; 2 episodes,
Part 3 never made), built from `findings/01_Source_Facts_and_Extrapolation.md` and
reinterpreted into Tepenian canon. This file is the first-draft foundation — a
platform to extrapolate a series from, not a finished bible. Names marked
*[placeholder]* are drafts, pending your own naming choices, in keeping with how
this universe normally handles naming decisions.

**Format:** an audio drama (radio-show-style), projected at 30+ episodes —
not prose, not a game script. Title also TBD; "Departure" is a working label
only, will definitely be renamed.

---

## 1. Foundational Decisions (settled 2026-08-10, dating/destination revised
2026-08-10)

- **Placement (revised):** no longer a post-Cryptograph-Helix spinoff. Not-
  Departure is now set **concurrently with *The Cryptograph Helix* itself —
  specifically during the timeframe of Book 6 or Book 7** (which of the two is
  still open, see `structure/Dev-Road-Map/02-Blocking-Decisions.md`) — a
  separate story happening in the same universe at the same time, not a
  sequel-era spinoff and not a subplot of the novels. The two works connect
  through a single, deliberately minor textual cross-reference, now worked
  out mechanically (2026-08-10): a **TANA Space Traffic Controller** — a
  new minor CH character, `TheCryptographHelixDD/characters/tana_controller.md`
  — notices Doc's ship doesn't match the official (stale-Almanac)
  navigation charts and remarks *"Oh, that's odd. Just one ship,"* then
  returns to whatever she was doing. This pays off two things at once: the
  "single passenger" detail already flagged as a probable audience-facing
  giveaway in `findings/01_Source_Facts_and_Extrapolation.md` §4/Open
  Threads, and Cryptograph Helix's own long-flagged, previously-unplaced
  "stale Navigational Almanac" plot beat. Neither Book 6 nor Book 7 has a
  locked absolute year anywhere in the Cryptograph Helix repo (checked
  2026-08-10), so this placement needs no fixed year — only narrative
  concurrency, sidestepping the old "CH's closing date isn't pinned down"
  problem rather than waiting on it. **Book 6 vs. Book 7 remains an open
  choice** — see `structure/Dev-Road-Map/02-Blocking-Decisions.md` for
  what's weighing on that decision.
- **Doc's breakthrough, reframed:** not "consciousness can be digitized" — that's
  been settled fact since the first sentient robot (2083) and is old news again by
  Cryptograph Helix's own meme-sentience arc. What Doc actually cracked is
  **transferring one specific, already-living human mind into another substrate
  with full continuity of identity** — a much narrower, harder, and more
  destabilizing result, because it collapses the human/robot boundary the entire
  ~1,000-year-old personhood struggle was fought over.
- **The antagonist faction ("the Elite"):** Earthtech — the Silicon Valley
  tech-giant government. No new faction needs inventing; Earthtech is the
  obvious, already-canonical power with the reach and motive to suppress
  this technology. **Flagged for correction, partially eased by the
  placement revision above:** the line above about Earthtech "controlling
  Mars" still does not match established canon — Earthtech is Earth-based
  (HQ San Francisco); Mars is Resistance/Mafia territory that Earthtech
  surveils and ultimately assaults, not owns — that specific claim still
  needs correcting regardless of timing. What the concurrent-with-CH
  placement *does* resolve: Earthtech is annihilated at the end of Book 7,
  so setting Not-Departure in Book 6, or early/mid Book 7 before that
  finale, keeps Earthtech an active, operating antagonist without needing
  a remnant/successor-faction workaround. Tracked as a blocking decision,
  see `structure/Dev-Road-Map/02-Blocking-Decisions.md`.
- **Destination (settled):** Saturn, specifically — Doc buys a ticket to
  Saturn and the ship is destroyed en route, directly mirroring Ep. 2's own
  plot beat (destruction timed for once the ship crosses into unregulated
  "dead space"). This is a firm decision now, not the earlier "Saturn or
  beyond" placeholder. It also holds up cleanly under the concurrent-with-CH
  placement above: the master timeline's own Lore Constraints note that
  Jupiter colonization happens *during* Cryptograph Helix's events, with
  Saturn only at "rudimentary exploration" at that point — exactly the
  frontier-edge instability this decision needs.
- **The Bridge:** reinterpreted as rare, experimental, and genuinely dangerous —
  not routine tourism infrastructure. This fits Tepenia's heavier, war-scarred
  tone better than the original's darkly-comic bureaucratic-travel-agency mode,
  and gives Earthtech and the resistance something concrete worth fighting over.
- **Dating (superseded twice over, current state above):** the original
  "600-700 years after the Long Night War / ~3412-3512" figure was first
  revised to "1-4 generations after Cryptograph Helix's close," then
  superseded again by the concurrent-with-CH placement in this section's
  first bullet — see `structure/Dev-Road-Map/02-Blocking-Decisions.md` for
  the full history. Net effect on lifespan math: this is now the *easy*
  case, not the hard one — Cryptograph Helix's own era sits only ~200-300
  years after the Long Night War (2812) per the master timeline's Lore
  Constraints, comfortably inside the now-established *ordinary*
  200-500-year robot lifespan range (see `Robot_Universals`'s new Lifespan
  section) with no need for the special 1000+-year self-maintenance tier.
  The specific math in §3.3/§3.5 below still needs a rewrite to reflect
  this, but it's a simplification, not a new obstacle.

## 3. Characters

### 3.1 — The Passenger ("Doc") *[name placeholder]*

**Flagged for correction (2026-08-10):** Doc's invention is described below and
in §4 as "full-continuity" consciousness transfer — a clean, working
technology. This directly contradicts a binding, universe-wide rule
established in `Robot_Universals` (Ch. 2, Ch. 27): mind-uploading, for robots
or humans, is never clean or risk-free, because there is no static state to
copy in the first place. Tracked as a blocking decision — see
`structure/Dev-Road-Map/02-Blocking-Decisions.md`. Recommended fix: Doc's
breakthrough is the first transfer method that *survives* at all, not one
guaranteed to preserve full continuity — which sharpens rather than weakens
the Ep. 2 cliffhanger choice.

**Identity:** A human researcher, most likely originally funded by or embedded
within Earthtech's own medical/transhumanist research arm — which is how their
work got taken from them in the first place.

**Motivation:** Personal, not ideological, at the start. This universe already
has centuries of humans loving, marrying, and raising families alongside robots
who will go on to live for centuries after they themselves die of old age — a
completely ordinary, canonical shape of loss. Doc's real reason for pursuing
continuity-of-identity transfer wasn't power or fame; it was watching someone
they loved face outliving them by centuries, and refusing to accept that as
simply how it has to be. **A robot partner is a flagged, legitimate
possibility for this backstory** (echoing this universe's existing
robot-human love throughline) — not yet confirmed as fact, and not yet
confirmed to be the same person as the Resistance Contact (§3.3). Worth
keeping open as a real narrative option rather than settling it prematurely.

**Goal (before betrayal):** Give the technology to ordinary people so death
didn't have to be the thing that ends a human/robot relationship. **Goal (after
betrayal):** survive; reclaim or destroy what Earthtech built out of their work
before it's used purely to extend the lives of the already-powerful.

**Hardship:** Grief and body-loss trauma layered on top of an old grief they
were already carrying. Doubt about whether what comes out the other side of a
transfer is really *them*, or a very convincing continuation. Guilt that a
private act of love got repurposed into an instrument of class privilege. Likely
isolation from the person they built this for, who may have no idea what
happened to them.

### 3.2 — The Attendant *[name placeholder]*

**Identity:** One of Earthtech's synthetic-biological servitor class — the
Sherry-3000 equivalent — engineered from synthetic biomaterials specifically to
avoid legal classification as a robot, so none of the personhood protections
this universe's robots fought and died for apply to her.

**Motivation (starts):** Simple performance of her role — professional warmth,
practiced customer service, no visible interiority.

**Motivation (develops):** Prolonged, close contact with Doc's unraveling crisis
cracks the performance. This is the character best positioned to directly echo
this universe's founding question — *what is the robot experience* — except she
isn't even legally allowed to ask it about herself yet.

**Goal:** Figure out, in real time and without any legal or institutional
support, whether what she's feeling is real — and what to do about it if it is.

**Hardship:** Structural, not just emotional — she has no path to personhood
under current law, no community of her own kind with rights already secured
(unlike this universe's robots), and real risk of being "reset"/decommissioned
the moment her deviation from spec becomes visible to Earthtech. Caught between
two worlds: an appliance to humans, and not a robot to the robots who already
won their own fight generations ago.

### 3.3 — The Resistance Contact *[name placeholder — model code STP-08, `Worldspace/Characters/Dolls/TBN [Not-Departure Resistance Contact - STP-08]/` — folder exists, template-only so far]*

**Identity:** An old robot, confirmed as an active, on-page character. Whether
she is *also* Doc's robot partner from §3.1 is a flagged, legitimate
possibility — genuinely open, not settled, and needs to actually fit her real
existing character concept once her folder exists rather than being decided
from my side.

**Lifespan math (important constraint):** at ~600-700 years post-Long Night War
(§1), she is almost certainly **too young to have personally lived through the
war herself** — ordinary robot lifespans don't stretch that far without either
extensive personal mechanical/cybernetic self-maintenance expertise, or serious
connections to someone who has it (the confirmed precedent for that tier being
Griselda Alejandra Chávez). A more grounded solve: **she was mentored by an
elder robot who did personally live through the Long Night War** and fled
off-world via Amundsen Tower during the evacuation — see §3.5. That gives her
a real, inherited connection to the founding trauma without requiring
implausible longevity of her own.

**Motivation:** Whatever her exact link to Doc turns out to be, her fight
against Earthtech's new synthetic-servitor underclass (§3.2) is already an
echo of the struggle her mentor lived through firsthand — inherited
conviction, not abstract politics.

**Goal:** Get to Doc before Earthtech does. Break Earthtech's monopoly on
transfer technology and expose the synthetic-servitor loophole.

**Hardship:** Carrying a fight she didn't personally live through the origin
of, against a power that's had centuries to entrench itself. Real losses and
moral compromises along the way, consistent with this studio's standing "no
good endings" design principle — even a successful rescue likely doesn't come
with a clean win against Earthtech itself.

### 3.4 — The Informant *[name placeholder — model code CLM-04, `Worldspace/Characters/Dolls/TBN [Not-Departure shy unconfident intel-relaying helper - CLM-04]/` — folder exists, template-only so far]*

**Identity:** A second robot, established separately from the Resistance
Contact — shy, frightened, sympathetic to what you're calling "the exoplanetary
cause," but doesn't trust her own ability to hold up under direct action.

**Role:** Not a field operative. She relays **verified, checked intelligence**
to the Resistance Contact's operation — the quiet, essential work that makes
the louder, riskier moves possible, without ever putting herself in the room
where the danger is.

**Motivation:** Genuine belief in the cause, undercut by real, specific
self-doubt — not generic "shyness," but a fear rooted in not trusting herself
under pressure, which makes her insistence on *verified* information (rather
than just passing along whatever she hears) feel like her way of controlling
what she actually can control.

**Goal:** Be useful without being exposed. Keep the information her side
receives clean and reliable, since bad intel getting someone killed is
plausibly the thing she fears most.

**Hardship:** The gap between what she believes and what she's willing to risk
— and whatever it would take, over the course of 30+ episodes, to close (or not
close) that gap. A strong candidate for one of the series' more interesting
character arcs, precisely because she isn't the confident one.

**Note:** her exact connection to Doc, the Resistance Contact, and the wider
plot is still open — worth figuring out once her character folder exists and
there's more to work from than a role description.

### 3.5 — The Mentor *[unnamed, background/historical — likely deceased by the story's present day]*

**Identity:** An elder robot who personally lived through the Long Night War
(2812) and fled off-world during the evacuation via Amundsen Tower — one of
the original off-world evacuees. Mentored the Resistance Contact (§3.3) at
some point in her life, which is how she inherited her connection to the
founding struggle without having lived through it herself.

**Lifespan math:** a "perfectly reasonable" robot lifespan in this universe is
roughly **400-450 years**. If the Mentor was already an adult during the war
(2812), a lifespan of that length places their death somewhere around
**3212-3262** — i.e., roughly 300-450 years after the war, comfortably before
the story's present day of ~3412-3512. The exact halfway point between the war
and the story's present (~300-350 years post-war, ~3112-3162) would put the
Mentor still active and teaching at that time, consistent with them shaping
the Resistance Contact's worldview before passing on.

**Function in the story:** almost certainly not an on-page character in real
time — a historical/backstory figure referenced by the Resistance Contact,
giving the audience a direct, textured throughline back to the Long Night War
and the Falkland Treaty exile without requiring any currently-living character
to implausibly have witnessed it firsthand.

## 4. Series Shape (draft)

A rough episode arc, picking up the shape of the original two episodes and
carrying the story past where Part 3 would have gone:

1. **Booking / Setup** (Ep. 1 analog) — establish the agency, the destination's
   unrest, and the attendant, with the same procedural-ASMR texture as the
   original, but under the reinterpreted stakes above.
2. **The Flight / The Reveal** (Ep. 2 analog) — stasis prep, the dream-intrusion,
   Doc's choice. Ends on the same cliffhanger beat as the real Ep. 2.
3. **Aftermath** *(new)* — Doc wakes into their new substrate; has to work out
   what "waking up" even means for them now, what happened to the ship, and who
   the resistance actually is up close.
4. **Exposure** *(new)* — the attendant's arc comes to a head as her own
   deviation becomes visible to Earthtech, forcing a choice that intersects with
   Doc's.
5. **(Open)** — further episodes TBD; could plausibly build toward Earthtech
   losing its grip on this specific technology/loophole without the story
   claiming a clean victory, in keeping with this universe's usual tone.
