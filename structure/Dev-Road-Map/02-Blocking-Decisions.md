# Blocking Decisions

Open either/or decisions that need resolving before downstream work
(naming, episode structure, character finalization) can proceed cleanly.

## 🔴 Mind-uploading is a hard "no clean transfer" universal — directly hits the premise

Confirmed by direct read of `TepenianUniverseTimeline/Reference/Robot_Universals/1_-_Part_I_Ground-Level_Fundamentals/02_The_Gel_Brain/README.md` (Ch. 2) and `.../04_Mortality_and_Integrity/README.md` (Ch. 4), 2026-08-10. This is a **binding, cross-media rule**, not a robot-only detail — Ch. 2 states it applies equally to humans:

> "Treat 'uploading a robot mind' as equally unrealistic, for the equally rigorous reason, as uploading a human mind — in this book, and in any future work anywhere in the Tepenian multimedia universe."

Chapter 27 (`4_-_Part_IV.../27_Cross-Media_Application/`) locks this in as a fact that "may never be silently contradicted by a later work," and names the Cryptograph Helix novels and Outer Tepenia by name as bound by it — i.e. everything on Not-Departure's own timeline.

**The mechanism, not just the rule:** a gel brain (and by the same logic, a human brain) has no static state to copy — cognition is continuously shifting, so there is no clean snapshot to "read out." This means substrate transfer can be *attempted* but must always carry real risk of memory loss or alteration — never portrayed as clean, guaranteed, or risk-free.

**Why this is blocking, not just flavor:** Doc's whole premise, as currently drafted (`../../Tepenian_Reinterpretation.md` §3.1, §4), is "full-continuity consciousness transfer... with full continuity of identity" — described as a clean, working breakthrough. That directly contradicts this universal. Two ways to resolve, not mutually exclusive:

1. **Reframe the invention** — Doc's breakthrough is the first transfer method that *survives* the transfer at all (vs. every prior attempt failing outright), not a "full-continuity, no-loss" one. The dream-cliffhanger choice (leave the body vs. die with the ship) gets sharper, not weaker, if "yes, but you might not come out the other side as fully yourself" is baked into the choice itself.
2. **Deliberate AU divergence** — treat Doc's tech as breaking this universal on purpose, same category of decision as the Earthtech/Mars item below.

Given this constraint is stated as binding *precisely to prevent exactly this kind of unreconciled contradiction* from a sibling project, (1) is the recommended default — flagged here rather than decided unilaterally.

**Superseded 2026-08-10 — robot lifespan is now real, sourced canon,** established directly and now recorded in `TepenianUniverseTimeline/Reference/Robot_Universals/1_-_Part_I_Ground-Level_Fundamentals/04_Mortality_and_Integrity/README.md`'s new "Lifespan" section: no fixed cap; **200-500 years** with ordinary competent maintenance; **1000+ years** for robots with deep mechanical/"cybernetic blacksmithing" self-maintenance skill (named precedents: Griselda Alejandra Chávez, Maggie Aarden). Not immortal — the same gel-brain/power-source death threshold still applies regardless of skill or age. The old "400-450 years" figure in `../../Tepenian_Reinterpretation.md` §3.5 and `../../background notes/characters/Mentor.md` should be read as this project's own earlier estimate, now superseded by the real 200-500/1000+ split above — those files still need a pass to update the number itself (not just the math built on it).

**Net effect on the Mentor/Resistance-Contact question:** this makes the "she's too young to have lived through the war" framing *less* certain than before, not more — 200-500 years easily spans from the Long Night War (2812) to well past Cryptograph Helix's own era, and a robot with real self-maintenance skill could plausibly still be alive from 2812 all the way to Not-Departure's present regardless of how the dating question above resolves. Whether the Resistance Contact (or the Mentor) actually falls into the skilled 1000+-year tier, or the ordinary 200-500 tier, is a real characterization choice now — not a math-forced conclusion either way.

**Confirmed 2026-08-10:** the 1000+-year tier also extends to a robot who doesn't personally have this skill but has reliable, ongoing access to someone who does (not a one-off favor). This is exactly what `../../Tepenian_Reinterpretation.md` §3.3 already hedged toward — "extensive personal mechanical/cybernetic self-maintenance expertise, **or serious connections to someone who has it**" — so the original bible's phrasing was already correctly anticipating this rule before it was confirmed. Strengthens the case that the Resistance Contact's own longevity (via her connection to the Mentor, if the Mentor holds this skill) is worth treating as a live option, not just the Mentor's.

case, not the hard one.

## 🔴 Earthtech's territorial claim

The reinterpretation currently states Earthtech "controls Mars" (carried
over from the original draft's framing of Earthtech as Cryptograph Helix's
Mars-controlling power). Established Cryptograph Helix canon says
otherwise: **Mars is Resistance/Mafia territory that Earthtech surveils
and later militarily assaults, not owns** — Earthtech is Earth-based (HQ
San Francisco). Two ways to resolve:

1. **Correct it** — reframe Earthtech's power base as Earth, with
   Not-Departure's setting (Saturn-or-beyond, Port of Phobos as a
   waypoint) staying outside Earthtech's home territory but within its
   reach, the same way it reaches toward Mars in the novels.
2. **Deliberate AU divergence** — keep Earthtech as a Mars power for this
   project specifically, treated as an intentional break from Cryptograph
   Helix canon rather than an error.

Also relevant: Cryptograph Helix's Book 7 ends with Earthtech
**annihilated** and Earth resettled by the Hungarians. If Not-Departure
sits only 1-4 generations after Cryptograph Helix's close, its Earthtech
is either operating *before* that collapse or is a **remnant/successor
faction operating after it** — this needs to be reflected explicitly
rather than assumed away, given how close the timelines now sit.
