# The Full Series: Constraint Specifications

2026-11-26. **Not the beats themselves.** The attributes each series-level beat has to have, and the ones it can't, across the whole series. Tags and weights: see `README.md`. **The episode count is undecided** (author, 2026-11-26). Positions are percentages; the episode numbers beside them are an example for a 30-episode run only.

**Working from (established):**
- A serialized audio drama, radio-play style, reinterpreting *Departure* (2013) into the Tepenian universe; title a placeholder.
- Set concurrently with *The Cryptograph Helix*, during Book 6 or Book 7 (which one is open), while the Earth government still holds power.
- Doc's ship, bound for Saturn, is to be destroyed once it crosses into "dead space."
- A TANA space-traffic controller's "Oh, that's odd. Just one ship" is the single cross-reference to the novels.
- The main cast is Doc (human researcher), the Attendant (synthetic-biological servitor, deliberately not legally a robot), the Resistance Contact (STP-08, old robot), and the Informant (CLM-04, robot). The Mentor (elder robot) is background only.

## Rules that hold everywhere

### MUST be true
1. **The main character is a robot** [Law: "The Main Character Is Always a Robot"]. **Settled (author, 2026-11-26): Doc becomes a robot.** The robot helping him transfers his mind into a robot body in Episode 2, so the lead is a robot from then on. Doc gets the full composite structure, as every foreground robot does [Composite].
2. **Every event after the series' Catalyst follows causally from what came before.** Only the Catalyst may be a coincidence [Law].
3. **Consciousness transfer is never clean** [RU, Ch. 2 and Ch. 27, binding on every Tepenian work]. A mind has no static state to copy; every transfer risks memory loss or alteration. Doc's breakthrough is at most the first method that *survives* the transfer, not one that preserves full continuity (`02-Blocking-Decisions.md`, recommended fix).
4. **No Good Endings governs the endings** (the "studio's standing principle," `Tepenian_Reinterpretation.md` §3.3): even a successful rescue does not come with a clean win against the Earth government.
5. **The Earth government stays active and powerful throughout**, because the series runs before its fall in Cryptograph Helix Book 7 [Adopted; Continuity].
6. **Every foreground robot gets the archetype layer** (the method's Step 8), Doc included from the transfer on; how many archetype arcs an audio series supports per character is undecided [Composite; Open].
7. **Every beat is legible by ear**: who is speaking, where we are, what just happened [Medium].
8. **Fidelity to the original:** its on-screen facts hold unless this repo's reinterpretation deliberately changes one, and every such change is recorded [Source; Adopted].

### CANNOT be true
1. **Any constraint built from the audience guides** [Law, 2026-11-27].
2. **A second coincidence anywhere in the series**, or a coincidence that gets anyone out of trouble [Law; Composite].
3. **A clean, risk-free transfer**, for Doc, for the Earth government's leaders, or anyone else [RU].
4. **Robots' personhood reopened as a question.** It was settled centuries earlier (Jeju-do 2318; Falkland Treaty 2564). The servitors' personhood is a live question precisely because they were built to fall outside it [Continuity].
5. **A named national mafia, or any beat that rests on a faction's real-world nationality** [Law: No National Stereotypes; Continuity].
6. **A currency named**, until the Tepenian Federation's economy has its own pass [Adopted].
7. **The Earth government shown controlling Mars**, unless the Cryptograph Helix settles that it does at this point (`02-Blocking-Decisions.md`) [Continuity].
8. **The Not-Departure story becoming a subplot of the novels.** One minor cross-reference only [Adopted].

## 1. Opening Image (Ep. 1, opening)
### MUST be true
1. **It sets the tone of the whole series**: the original's calm, procedural, customer-service texture, under stakes the listener doesn't yet know [Snyder; Source].
2. **It is the "before" picture** the Closing Image will reverse [Snyder; Composite].
### CANNOT be true
1. **Revealing the plot behind the booking** [Snyder; Source: the reveal belongs to Ep. 2].

## 2. Theme Stated (~5%; Ep. 1–2)
### MUST be true
1. **Said to the protagonist, casually and in passing, by someone else** [Composite; Snyder]. The original's agent and attendant speak in sales copy and service lines; the statement can hide in exactly that register [Proposed].
2. **Understood only later** [Snyder].
### CANNOT be true
1. **A stated moral** [Composite].

## 3. Set-Up (0–10%; Ep. 1–3)
### MUST be true
1. **The A-story cast is brought on or pointed toward** [Snyder].
2. **A Care Package is planted**, the setup half of the Q-Factor pair [Composite; Bell].
3. **The world's integration is shown**: identity, medical history and money all keyed to a retinal scan [Source]. It is also why no one disappears easily [Proposed, from `findings/01` §2].
4. **Fair clues are planted, hidden in plain procedure**: a single passenger; an unusually invasive background check [Source; Proposed: the "giveaway on rewatch"].
### CANNOT be true
1. **The listener told the danger outright before the Catalyst** [Snyder].

## 4. Catalyst (~10%; Ep. 2–3)
### MUST be true
1. **It comes from outside the protagonist** [Truby 4; Snyder]. The dream intrusion and the planned destruction of the ship qualify [Source].
2. **It is the series' one permitted coincidence, if it needs one** [Law]. As drafted it needs none: the resistance found Doc through his own booking after weeks of searching, and the single-passenger flight is the Earth government's doing [Source].
3. **It lands the protagonist in deeper trouble while seeming to offer escape** [Truby 4]: the transfer is a way out of a dying body into an unknown state [Source].

### CANNOT be true
1. **Caused by the protagonist's own choice** [Truby 4].

## 5. Debate and Break into Two (10–20%; Ep. 3–6)
### MUST be true
1. **The Break into Two is a choice the protagonist makes freely** [Snyder]. **Settled (author, 2026-11-26):** Doc's upload in Episode 2, made under the threat of certain death, is the Catalyst's consequence, not the crossing. The Break into Two comes after he wakes (around Episodes 3–6), when he **chooses** to join the resistance's fight [Adopted].
2. **Clear-cut and irreversible** [Composite; Bell].
3. **The Debate's question is answered first**: should I, can I, and who are these people up close? [Snyder; `Tepenian_Reinterpretation.md` §4, "Aftermath"]. It runs while Doc is learning what it is to be in a robot body [Adopted; Proposed].
### CANNOT be true
1. **The protagonist carried into the fight by circumstance alone** [Snyder].
2. **The new substrate's uncertainty resolved before the crossing**: "is what woke up really me?" stays open [Proposed; from Doc's core fear].

## 6. B-Story (from ~27%; Ep. 7 → the Midpoint)
### MUST be true
1. **It seems unrelated, until it converges at the Midpoint** [Composite].
2. **It is the series' "love story" in the broad sense, and the forum for its theme** [Snyder]. Candidates already in the files: the Attendant's question of whether what she feels is real; Doc's lost love (possibly a robot partner) [Proposed].
3. **Its carrier is present from its start to its convergence** [CDM].
### CANNOT be true
1. **Telegraphed early** [Composite].

## 7. Road of Trials (20–50%; Ep. 6–15)
### MUST be true
1. **It delivers the promise of the premise**: the Bridge, exotic matter, the servitor loophole, a body that may not be one's own [Snyder; Adopted: exotic matter as load-bearing].
2. **The first plan fails** [Truby 10].
3. **The opponent's power is shown directly at the First Pinch Point** (~37%; Ep. 11) [Weiland].
4. **What is gained here saves the day at the end** [Harmon].
### CANNOT be true
1. **The stakes peaking before the Midpoint** [Snyder].

## 8. Midpoint (50%; Ep. 15)
### MUST be true
1. **Up or down is decided before the rest** [Snyder; Composite].
2. **The stakes rise; the A- and B-stories converge** [Snyder; Composite].
3. **A Mirror Moment** ("Who am I? What have I become? What must I become?"), which for a transferred mind is literal [Composite; Bell; Proposed].
4. **The inverse of All Is Lost** [Snyder].
### CANNOT be true
1. **Neither up nor down** [Snyder].

## 9. Bad Guys Close In (50–75%; Ep. 15–22)
### MUST be true
1. **The Earth government regroups and comes back harder; the protagonists' side fractures from inside** [Snyder; Composite].
2. **Its pressure stays immediate** even though its home is Earth and the story is out at the frontier [Snyder, "Watch Out for That Glacier!"].
3. **The Second Pinch Point (~62%; Ep. 19) shows its power directly** [Weiland].
### CANNOT be true
1. **A weak opponent** [Snyder].
2. **The Earth government's leaders learning or changing** [Continuity with the Cryptograph Helix: it acknowledges no Shadow].

## 10. All Is Lost and Dark Night (75–80%; Ep. 22–24)
### MUST be true
1. **The only moment in the series that plainly looks like the end** [Truby 14].
2. **A whiff of death** [Composite; Snyder]. In a story about transferring minds, a death can be literal, or the loss of a self [Proposed].
3. **It matches the protagonist's established Greatest Fear** [CDM]. For Doc as drafted: that what survived the transfer isn't really him [Proposed].
4. **The Q-Factor is a callback to the Care Package; the Moral Decision comes from an established flaw** [Composite].
### CANNOT be true
1. **A rescue by luck** [Law].

## 11. Break into Three (~80%; Ep. 24)
### MUST be true
1. **The solution appears**, from the B-Story fused with the A-story [Snyder; Composite].
2. **A discovery or setback makes the climax reachable** [Bell].
### CANNOT be true
1. **An opponent's move with no protagonist's answer attached** [Snyder].

## 12. Finale (80–100%; Ep. 24–30)
### MUST be true
1. **The ending is costly, not clean** [No Good Endings; `Tepenian_Reinterpretation.md` §4.5: the Earth government loses its grip on this technology or loophole, "without the story claiming a clean victory"].
2. **The opposition falls from the bottom up**, as far as it falls: the Earth government itself falls in the novels, not here [Snyder; Continuity].
3. **Every setup pays off**, the dream-simulation menu included if it is kept as foreshadowing [Snyder].
4. **The battle is over values** [Truby 19].
### CANNOT be true
1. **The Earth government destroyed in this series.** Its annihilation belongs to Cryptograph Helix Book 7 [Continuity].
2. **A deus ex machina** [CDM].
3. **A purely good ending** [No Good Endings].

## 13. Closing Image (Ep. 30)
### MUST be true
1. **It reverses the Opening Image** [Snyder; Composite]. The original opens on a calm booking for a trip; the reversal has that same frame transformed [Proposed].
### CANNOT be true
1. **An image unrelated to the opening** [Snyder].
