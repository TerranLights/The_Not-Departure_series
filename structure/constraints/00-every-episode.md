# Every Episode: Constraint Specifications

2026-11-26. **Not the beats themselves.** What every episode must and must not be, as its own story layer inside the serialized whole. Each episode is judged within its own layer. Tags and weights: see `README.md`.

**Why episodes are a layer of their own:** Harmon's circle was built for short, episodic work. In television, he says, every episode completes all eight steps even while the series as a whole moves slowly (Harmon, Story Structure 105). A serialized audio drama works the same way, whatever its length.

---

## MUST be true
1. **Each episode completes its own circle**, You, Need, Go, Search, Find, Take, Return, Change, even if some steps are only implied [Harmon]. Each step can be as small as the episode needs [Harmon, "major is relative"].
2. **Step 8 carries change forward.** Harmon notes that sitcom television resets at step 8 as a commercial convention; a serial does the opposite. Each episode leaves the situation permanently changed [Harmon].
3. **It closes its own question and opens a new one in the same beat**, so the next episode is pulled in [Fox 8]. The original's Episode 2, ending mid-transfer, is the model [Source].
4. **At least one revelation per episode**: the listener learns something about the characters, the world or the plot that they didn't know before [Fox 4].
5. **The pleasures promised early keep coming**: the procedural, close, second-person ASMR texture of the original, wherever the story allows it [Fox 7; Source].
6. **Legible by ear**: speaker, place and action are carried by dialogue, foley and narration [Medium].
7. **A tentpole moment, or a deliberate quieter pacing episode**, never neither [Fox 6].
8. **It advances the series**: the episode serves its place in the series structure (`00-series.md`), judged there, not here.

## CANNOT be true
1. **An episode that only explains.** Exposition rides under something worth hearing [Snyder, the Pope in the Pool].
2. **Information only a viewer could get.** A look, a sign, a silent gesture with no sound or description [Medium].
3. **A step-8 reset that undoes a change the series depends on** [Harmon; Continuity].
4. **A coincidence that gets anyone out of trouble** [Law; Composite].
5. **Breaking the listener's footing without a reason.** The original keeps the listener inside one person's experience. Hopping viewpoint for more than about a quarter of an episode loses the listener [Harmon, on viewpoint hopping; Proposed for audio].
